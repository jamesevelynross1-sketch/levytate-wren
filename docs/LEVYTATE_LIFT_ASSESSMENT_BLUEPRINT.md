# LevyTate Lift Workforce Capability Assessment — pilot instrument

> Review status: original pilot candidate. Software verification does not make this a scientifically validated, accredited or occupational-competence assessment.

Framework: `lift-workforce-v1`
Questionnaire: `lift-workforce-questionnaire-v1`
Interpretation rules: `lift-workforce-rules-v1`

The instrument contains three unscored context prompts and 30 substantive prompts across six workplace episodes. It produces evidence states—supported, emerging, insufficient or contradictory—not a score out of 100, pass/fail result or employee ranking.

## Assessment content

### Before you begin

#### intro_responsibilities — Which responsibilities are most relevant to your work at the moment?

Describe the work context only. This is not scored and does not need personal information.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: unscored_context. Unscored context only.

#### intro_interests — What would you most like to get better at or understand more clearly?

Development interests are reflected back to you but never treated as capability evidence.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: unscored_context. Unscored context only.

#### intro_opportunity — Where, if anywhere, could you apply new learning in your current work?

It is fine to say that there is no current opportunity.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: unscored_context. Unscored context only.

### A convincing answer that needs checking

#### check_01 — A recommendation is clear and confident, but one source covers only a small part of the affected work. What is the strongest next step?

Choose the most defensible check before a decision is made.

- A. Accept the recommendation because it is clearly written
- B. Check the source population, find what is missing and state the uncertainty
- C. Reject the recommendation because one source is incomplete
- D. Ask for a longer recommendation using the same evidence

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Think clearly; Make information useful.

#### check_02 — An AI assistant drafted the recommendation from supplied notes. Which control matters most before it is used?

Assume the tool is approved, but the decision still matters.

- A. Use it unchanged because the tool is approved
- B. Verify claims against authorised sources and keep a named human decision owner
- C. Ask the AI to certify that its own answer is correct
- D. Remove all detail so checking is unnecessary

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Work intelligently with AI; Protect trust.

#### check_03 — Write two or three sentences explaining the recommendation, the main uncertainty and the next check to a colleague who has not seen the evidence.

Clarity is more important than formal writing style. Do not include confidential information.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: short_work_sample. Capabilities: Make yourself understood; Think clearly.

#### check_04 — The decision is time-sensitive. Which response balances progress and evidence best?

Assume delaying indefinitely also has a cost.

- A. Make the full change immediately
- B. Run a bounded test with an owner, decision date and clear stop conditions
- C. Wait until every uncertainty can be eliminated
- D. Proceed quietly so no one can delay it

Evidence type: interpretation. Capabilities: Turn priorities into progress; Create lasting value.

#### check_05 — When new evidence has challenged your first view, what have you usually done?

Choose the closest answer. Lack of opportunity is recorded as unknown, not weak.

- A. I have not had enough opportunity to show this
- B. I usually keep the first approach to avoid disruption
- C. I review the evidence and adjust with some support
- D. I can explain what changed and revise the approach independently

Evidence type: anchored_experience. Capabilities: Grow through change.

### An improvement that moves the problem elsewhere

#### flow_01 — A proposed shortcut reduces one team's handling time but creates extra checking for another team. What should happen next?

Consider the whole flow, not just the first team.

- A. Approve it because the first team's metric improves
- B. Map the affected handover and compare the total work and service outcome
- C. Reject all process changes that affect another team
- D. Ask the second team to absorb the extra work

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: See better ways; Create lasting value.

#### flow_02 — Which evidence would best test whether the change improved the whole process?

Select the measure that avoids a local-only conclusion.

- A. Time saved by the first team only
- B. End-to-end elapsed time, rework and service quality for a defined population and period
- C. How positively the project team describes the change
- D. The number of messages sent during implementation

Evidence type: interpretation. Capabilities: Make information useful; Turn priorities into progress.

#### flow_03 — Describe a small test that could reveal downstream consequences before a wider change.

Include a scope, who needs to be involved and one condition for stopping or revising the test.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: short_work_sample. Capabilities: See better ways; Grow through change.

#### flow_04 — The receiving team says the proposal will make their work less reliable. What is the most constructive response?

Choose how to handle evidence and disagreement.

- A. Explain that efficiency matters more than their concern
- B. Ask for concrete examples, restate the shared outcome and include them in the test design
- C. Cancel the work without examining the concern
- D. Escalate their resistance as a performance issue

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Help people succeed; Make yourself understood.

#### flow_05 — How do you usually check who could be adversely affected by a process change?

Choose the closest answer. No opportunity is a valid response.

- A. I have not had enough opportunity to show this
- B. I focus on the team requesting the change
- C. I consider affected groups when someone prompts me
- D. I identify affected groups, information risks and safeguards as part of the change

Evidence type: anchored_experience. Capabilities: Protect trust.

### A team that sees the problem differently

#### team_01 — Two colleagues disagree about the cause of a recurring problem. What is the strongest first move?

The aim is a workable shared understanding, not forced agreement.

- A. Choose the more senior colleague's explanation
- B. Clarify what each person observed, separate facts from assumptions and identify a check
- C. Ask both people to compromise on a cause
- D. Avoid discussing the disagreement and allocate actions

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Make yourself understood; Help people succeed.

#### team_02 — The examples support more than one explanation. What conclusion is justified?

Choose the statement that preserves uncertainty.

- A. The explanation with the most examples is proven
- B. Several explanations remain plausible; gather discriminating evidence before assigning cause
- C. No action is possible until one explanation wins
- D. Both explanations must be equally true

Evidence type: interpretation. Capabilities: Think clearly; Help people succeed.

#### team_03 — Draft a short message that summarises what is agreed, what remains uncertain and the next commitment.

Use your own words. Writing style and spelling are not scored as separate capabilities.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: short_work_sample. Capabilities: Make yourself understood.

#### team_04 — One colleague holds knowledge needed for the next step but has limited time. What is the best approach?

Balance progress with sustainable knowledge sharing.

- A. Wait for that colleague to complete the whole task
- B. Agree the smallest useful contribution, capture the knowledge and assign the remaining work clearly
- C. Proceed without their input and correct mistakes later
- D. Make them permanently responsible for similar work

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Help people succeed; Turn priorities into progress.

#### team_05 — When a team commitment is made, how do you usually help it remain useful after the immediate issue?

Choose the closest answer; not enough opportunity is not a negative result.

- A. I have not had enough opportunity to show this
- B. I rely on people remembering the agreement
- C. I sometimes capture an owner or next step
- D. I make ownership, evidence, review and knowledge transfer explicit

Evidence type: anchored_experience. Capabilities: Create lasting value.

### Numbers that tell an incomplete story

#### data_01 — Reported errors fell from 40 to 30, while completed transactions fell from 1,000 to 500. Which interpretation is defensible?

Use the supplied numbers; no calculation tool is required.

- A. Quality improved because there were fewer errors
- B. The error count fell, but the error rate rose from 4% to 6%; volume and quality changed differently
- C. Quality declined by exactly 25%
- D. Nothing can be said about either volume or quality

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Make information useful; Think clearly.

#### data_02 — Which additional information is most important before comparing two teams' error rates?

Choose the information needed for a fair comparison.

- A. The teams' names
- B. Comparable definitions, populations, case complexity and reporting periods
- C. Which team has the newer system
- D. The total number of employees in the organisation

Evidence type: interpretation. Capabilities: Make information useful.

#### data_03 — Explain the change in errors and transactions without overstating what caused it.

Include the denominator and one limitation.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: short_work_sample. Capabilities: Make yourself understood; Protect trust.

#### data_04 — A leader asks for a single success number. What is the strongest response?

Keep the decision useful while preserving material context.

- A. Give the lower error count without qualification
- B. Provide a small set of defined measures covering volume, rate and service outcome, with the decision they inform
- C. Refuse to provide any summary
- D. Average all available measures into one index

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Create lasting value; Turn priorities into progress.

#### data_05 — When a measure changes meaning or its data quality is challenged, what do you usually do?

Choose the closest response.

- A. I have not had enough opportunity to show this
- B. I continue using the measure for consistency
- C. I note the issue and seek help to revise the conclusion
- D. I revisit the definition, preserve the old view and explain the impact of the change

Evidence type: anchored_experience. Capabilities: Grow through change.

### A new way of working

#### change_01 — You must use a small unfamiliar process tomorrow. What is the strongest preparation?

Assume a short guide and a knowledgeable colleague are available.

- A. Memorise the guide without trying the process
- B. Practise one representative case, note uncertainties and confirm the recovery path
- C. Wait until formal training is arranged
- D. Use the process on a high-risk case first

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Grow through change; Turn priorities into progress.

#### change_02 — The new process includes an automated suggestion. When should the suggestion be accepted?

The automation is intended to assist, not own the decision.

- A. Whenever it is generated successfully
- B. When relevant inputs and permissions are valid, exceptions are checked and the accountable person agrees
- C. When it saves more time than a manual check
- D. Only after the supplier confirms it is accurate

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Work intelligently with AI; Protect trust.

#### change_03 — During practice, a key assumption in the guide proves false. What is the best response?

Choose how to learn without hiding the problem.

- A. Work around it quietly
- B. Pause the affected step, record the mismatch, seek the right authority and update the test
- C. Abandon the entire process
- D. Continue because the guide is the approved source

Evidence type: interpretation. Capabilities: Grow through change; Think clearly.

#### change_04 — Write a brief handover note explaining the changed assumption and what the next person should do.

Include the boundary of what you know and who can authorise a change.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: short_work_sample. Capabilities: Make yourself understood; Help people succeed.

#### change_05 — How do you usually use feedback after trying a new way of working?

Choose the closest response.

- A. I have not had enough opportunity to show this
- B. I mainly use feedback to confirm whether people liked the change
- C. I make small adjustments when someone identifies an issue
- D. I compare feedback with the intended outcome and revise a bounded part of the process

Evidence type: anchored_experience. Capabilities: See better ways.

### Pressure to deliver

#### pressure_01 — Two urgent requests compete for the same time; one involves confidential information and a fixed external deadline. What should you do first?

Assume you cannot complete both safely without agreement.

- A. Start the request from the most senior person
- B. Clarify impact and deadline, protect the confidential work and agree a realistic priority or escalation
- C. Work on both at once without changing expectations
- D. Send the confidential material to a colleague to save time

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Turn priorities into progress; Protect trust.

#### pressure_02 — Which evidence best supports a decision about the priority?

Choose evidence connected to outcomes and risk.

- A. Who asked first
- B. Deadline, affected service or customer, harm of delay, dependencies and effort
- C. Which request is easiest
- D. How often each requester follows up

Evidence type: interpretation. Capabilities: Create lasting value; Make information useful.

#### pressure_03 — Draft a concise escalation that sets out the conflict, your proposed priority and the decision needed.

Do not include real confidential information.

Response: bounded free text. Writing style, spelling and disclosure of personal history are not separately scored.

Evidence type: short_work_sample. Capabilities: Make yourself understood; Help people succeed.

#### pressure_04 — An approved AI tool could speed up part of the urgent confidential task, but its permitted data classification is unclear. What is the strongest action?

Speed does not override permissions.

- A. Use it because the tool is approved
- B. Confirm the permitted data and use a safe alternative until authority is clear
- C. Remove a few names and assume the remaining content is safe
- D. Ask the AI whether the information is confidential

Evidence type: structured_judgement. Capabilities: Work intelligently with AI; Protect trust.

#### pressure_05 — After a pressured delivery, how do you usually turn feedback into a better future approach?

Choose the closest response.

- A. I have not had enough opportunity to show this
- B. I focus on whether the deadline was met
- C. I discuss what could change when a similar issue arises
- D. I capture outcome, trade-offs and a specific change with an owner and review point

Evidence type: anchored_experience. Capabilities: Grow through change; Create lasting value.
